# LocalScribe 0.2.0 — The UI Update

The mic bubble has been rebuilt. It shrinks out of the way when you are not using it, takes the colour and opacity you choose, stays on screen wherever you put it, and can be held down to dictate — press, speak, let go. Around it: a recording limit that means a forgotten recording can no longer crash the app, error messages written for people, a way to send feedback that carries none of your dictated text, and an honest refusal on processors too old to run the speech engine.

Everything still runs on your phone. Nothing you dictate leaves the device.

Installs over any earlier version from v0.1.1 on; every release shares one signing certificate.

## What's new

- **Hold to dictate.** Press and hold the bubble: recording starts at once, with an equalizer in place of the buttons. Let go and it transcribes — no check mark. A quick tap still gives you the cancel / confirm controls.
- **The bubble gets out of the way.** A few seconds after it appears it shrinks to a small dot. Tap the dot to bring it back. The delay is yours to set — immediately, up to a minute, or never.
- **Drag it to the X to hide it**, until the next text field or for 1 minute to an hour. The phone ticks when you reach the X and confirms when you drop it. LocalScribe's notification also has **Hide bubble** and **Show bubble**.
- **Your colour, your opacity.** Nine presets including white and black, and opacity from 15% to solid. The mic icon switches light or dark to stay readable; the red recording light always stays fully visible.
- **Recording limit.** Recordings stop on their own at 1, 2 (default), 3, 5 or 10 minutes, and what you said is transcribed and inserted, never thrown away.
- **Feedback from Settings.** Opens a pre-filled GitHub issue in your browser, showing you exactly what it contains first. It can never include your transcripts, vocabulary or audio.
- **An occasional star request.** Every 100 dictations, a small card asks whether you would star the project on GitHub. Say yes or no once and it never asks again.

## Bug fixes

- The recording controls expanded off the right edge of the screen when the bubble was near it.
- The ✕ used to hide the bubble, and a bubble dragged low, were hidden behind the keyboard.
- The bubble could be dragged under the status bar and the navigation bar.
- A square shadow showed behind the recording controls.
- An octagon-shaped shadow showed through the bubble behind the mic when opacity was below 100%.
- The cancel and confirm buttons were hard to tell apart from the controls behind them.
- A recording left running could exhaust memory after 20–40 minutes, killing the app and losing the dictation with no message.
- Audio stayed in memory after a normal finish until the next dictation began.
- Tapping the bubble when dictation could not start — no microphone permission, no speech model — did nothing visible, and the bubble then stayed stuck over every app.
- Error messages showed raw internal text such as `SQLITE_FULL` or `Unable to resolve host "huggingface.co"`.
- On processors older than about 2018 the app crashed instantly at every launch, with no message.
- Dictation did not pause media playback, and kept recording when a call took the microphone.

## Under the hood

For anyone reading the source or filing issues.

- **Overlay layout.** The bubble's position is one anchor — its centre in screen pixels. Every overlay shape (dot, bubble, tap pill, hold pill, spinner) is centred on it and clamped into a safe area: 16 dp from the sides, below the status bar, above the navigation bar and, while one is open, above the IME. Application overlays are drawn beneath the IME, so the accessibility service publishes the input-method window's top edge from `typeWindowsChanged` events. Windows are sized explicitly per shape plus shadow padding, replacing `WRAP_CONTENT` windows that grew from their top-left corner.
- **Shadows.** Opacity is applied through colours rather than a `graphicsLayer` alpha, which rendered offscreen at the layer's size and clipped shadows square. Shadows are drawn with the shape's outline clipped out (`clipOutPath` + `setShadowLayer`), because an elevation shadow is rendered under the whole shape and its polygonal core shows through any translucent fill.
- **Gestures.** One handler on the overlay's root decides tap, drag or hold by whichever comes first: release, touch slop, or 250 ms. It lives on the root because a hold swaps the bubble for the pill while the finger is still down. Drags use raw screen coordinates, since Compose's are relative to a window moving under the finger. Hold mode travels with the recording (`EXTRA_HOLD`), and a hold released within 400 ms becomes a tap recording rather than transcribing near-silence.
- **Collapse and dismissal.** The collapse timer is a pure reducer with a generation counter, so a superseded timer can never collapse the bubble early. Field focus and a finished dictation restart it through a counter, since moving between fields is `Idle → Idle` and a `StateFlow` does not re-emit an equal value. Timed dismissal is a wall-clock deadline that survives the process; one further out than the longest option is treated as expired.
- **Recording memory.** Capture is bounded on the write path by `RecordingBudget`, with chunked storage and a draining decode: peak memory fell from about 5× to 3× the recorded audio.
- **Processor check.** `/proc/cpuinfo` is read before any native library loads, and the check fails open on anything uncertain. Only positive evidence that the ARMv8.2 `asimdhp` or `asimddp` features are missing disables dictation.
- **Feedback privacy.** Reports are built from a `DeviceFacts` type with no field able to hold a transcript, vocabulary word, audio or exception message. Only exception class names are recorded. LocalScribe opens the URL in your browser and makes no network request itself: the only network call in the app is still the model download from `huggingface.co`.
- **Tests.** 278 unit tests, covering the overlay geometry, collapse reducer, colour contrast (every swatch keeps its icon at 3:1 or better), dismissal timing and star-prompt rules. The overlay's windows, gestures and haptics have no instrumented tests.

## Known limitations

- The overlay has only been used on one device, a Galaxy S25 Ultra on Android 16. The octagon fix was made after that and has not yet been seen on a device.
- Apps that deliberately hide overlays on sensitive screens (the Contacts editor, for example) hide the bubble too. That is the platform's choice, not something LocalScribe can work around.
- Processors older than ARMv8.2 are told they are unsupported. Dictation does not run on them.
